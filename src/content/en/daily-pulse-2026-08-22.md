## 2. JD.com Bets on ¥10 Billion and 80 RoboBases: Robotics Commercialization Is Becoming a Service-Network Contest

**In one sentence**: _JD.com says it will commit ¥10 billion in robotics resources by 2028, help 100 brands exceed ¥1 billion in standalone sales, build service coverage across more than 100 countries within five years and establish 80 RoboBase facilities._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: The significance is not simply that the investment sounds large. It is that the plan pulls robotics back from single-machine performance toward network capability. Once a robot enters a real business, it creates recurring needs for installation, calibration, maintenance, spare parts, remote diagnosis, software updates and operator training. Without that network, a robot remains a one-off equipment sale. With it, the category has a path toward repeatable infrastructure.

**Key commitments disclosed by JD.com**:
- **Resource commitment**: **¥10 billion** in robotics resources by 2028
- **Brand development**: help **100 brands** exceed **¥1 billion** in standalone sales
- **End-user reach**: move robotics into **one million end-user scenarios**
- **After-sales coverage**: service capability across **100+ countries** within five years
- **Service facilities**: **80 RoboBases** spanning display and delivery, repair, R&D design, pilot assembly, data collection, manufacturing and iteration
- **Employment**: more than **100,000 new robotics service-engineer jobs**

![JD.com presents its robotics strategy at the 2026 World Robot Conference](/images/daily-pulse-2026-08-22-jd-strategy.png)

The comparison framework is changing. Earlier product discussions focused on degrees of freedom, payload, battery life and model performance. Buyers deploying robots at scale will also ask how many service engineers are available in a region, how quickly parts can arrive, whether failures can be diagnosed remotely and whether upgrades require a factory return. The company that standardizes those answers will be better positioned for long-term contracts.

> 📌 **Conclusion**: JD.com's strategy reads like a map of robotics infrastructure. It does not prove immediate order conversion, but it shows that the industry is beginning to acknowledge a hard truth: the last mile of commercialization is decided by service density, not launch-event volume.

## 3. Ten Million Hours of Real-World Data and 10+ Million Connected Devices: Robotics Is Entering a Physical-World Data Race

**In one sentence**: _JD.com says JoyInside is expected to connect more than 10 million devices, including robot products, this year; JD Cloud plans to collect more than 10 million hours of real-world data over two years to support an independent AI foundation-model matrix._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: Robots face friction, occlusion, changing light, object weight, spontaneous human instructions and hard safety boundaries. Laboratory data can show that a model can complete a task. Real-world data is what teaches a system to complete it reliably under variation. For robotics companies, the data layer supports not only training, but also failure analysis, task decomposition, scenario transfer and service diagnostics.

**Key facts**:
- **Ecosystem reach**: more than **10 million connected devices** expected this year, including robot products
- **Real-world data**: more than **10 million hours** planned over two years
- **Data sources**: real retail, logistics, technology, healthcare, industrial and household environments
- **Commercial base**: JD.com says it already works with **200+ robotics brands** and many core-component suppliers through its direct retail business
- **International channel**: Joybuy has established cooperation with **50+ Chinese robotics brands**

![JD.com's robotics ecosystem and scenario-based rollout](/images/daily-pulse-2026-08-22-jd-ecosystem.png)

One boundary matters here: **a data-collection plan is not the same thing as proven model capability**. The value of 10 million hours depends on collection standards, privacy compliance, task coverage and the quality of the feedback loop. The useful metric is not hours alone. It is whether the resulting data lowers deployment cost, shortens debugging cycles and keeps robots stable across different stores, warehouses and homes.

> 📌 **Conclusion**: The next robotics contest may be fought over who has a sustained entry point into the physical world. Hardware determines whether a robot can move; data determines whether it can keep working when the environment changes.

## 4. Emergency Requirements and a Responsible-Robot Initiative: Application Growth Needs Governance

**In one sentence**: _The conference news center published “Application Requirements for Embodied Intelligence in Emergency Scenarios” and the “Initiative for the Upward and Beneficial Development of Intelligent Robots” on Aug. 20, placing deployment expansion and governance on the same agenda._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: Emergency response is one of the most valuable and difficult use cases for embodied intelligence. Robots may support reconnaissance, carrying, communications relay or assisted rescue in dangerous areas and complex public spaces. Those missions also raise harder questions about reliability, fail-safe behavior, human handover and responsibility. The industry cannot only ask what a robot can do. It must also define when the robot should stop, who can take control and what happens after a failure.

Publishing requirements and a responsible-development initiative together sends a clear signal: moving from demonstrations to broad public benefit is not only an engineering problem. It is also a governance problem. For buyers, safety certification, data boundaries, human override and after-sales accountability are becoming part of the product scorecard.

> 📌 **Conclusion**: As embodied intelligence expands its application radius, safety cannot be treated as a post-launch patch. Commercial speed and governance maturity have to move together.
