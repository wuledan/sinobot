## Key Takeaways

China's robot vacuum market underwent significant structural shifts in the first half of 2026. Market share concentration among the top four players (CR4) exceeded 85%, mopping technology reached a plateau where further premium investment yields diminishing returns, and the industry average selling price dropped from ¥3,200 in 2025 to ¥2,800 — a 12.5% decline driven by aggressive price competition at the ¥2,000-2,500 tier.

**The competitive landscape evolved from a "Big Three" to a "One Leader, Three Challengers" structure. Roborock leads with approximately 28% market share, Dreame follows closely at 24%, Ecovacs holds 18%, and Xiaomi's Mi Ecosystem brand captures 10% through channel leverage and aggressive pricing. Narwal maintains a niche at 8%.**

Total industry shipments in H1 2026 are projected to exceed 28 million units (including exports), representing approximately 22% year-over-year growth. The domestic market is approaching saturation, making overseas expansion the primary growth engine for every major brand. Roborock, Dreame, and Ecovacs all report overseas revenue exceeding 40% of total revenue.

### Domestic vs Export Breakdown

## Competitive Landscape Analysis

### Roborock: Market Leader with Full-Price-Layer Coverage

Roborock maintained its #1 domestic position through a comprehensive price-coverage strategy — from the entry-level G20 (¥2,199) to the flagship Qrevo Pro (¥4,999), spanning the full price spectrum.

**Key move:** In March 2026, Roborock released the P20 Pro series, bringing flagship-level LDS navigation and AI obstacle avoidance to the ¥2,499 price point for the first time. This directly challenged Xiaomi and Narwal in the mid-range segment, with the P20 Pro becoming the #1 SKU in the Tmall robot vacuum category during the June 18th shopping festival.

**Overseas performance:** Roborock's offline channel expansion in South Korea and Japan has yielded results. In Q1 2026, Roborock's South Korean market share surpassed 15%, making it the second-best performing Chinese brand in the country after Dreame.

**Concern:** Roborock lags behind competitors in AI voice interaction and smart home ecosystem integration. Compared to DreameGPT and Ecovacs' YIKO-GPT, Roborock's voice assistant remains at a basic command-control level. In the "AI + robot vacuum" brand perception battle, Roborock needs to accelerate its AI capabilities.

### Dreame: Fastest-Growing Brand, Mopping Technology Leader

Dreame was the fastest-growing brand in market share during H1 2026. After surpassing Ecovacs for the #2 position in domestic share in early 2025, Dreame continued to strengthen its position through both technological differentiation and aggressive overseas expansion.

**Core differentiator:** The X50 series biomimetic robotic arm mopping — the industry's only solution that achieves zero-dead-angle corner cleaning. This innovation gave Dreame a clearly defined premium positioning in the ¥4,500+ segment.

**DreameGPT:** In April 2026, Dreame launched DreameGPT — the industry's first LLM-based robot vacuum interaction system. DreameGPT supports natural-language cleaning commands, such as "re-mop under the sofa and vacuum the balcony dust," parsed and executed as a single compound instruction.

**Export leader:** Dreame executed the most aggressive overseas expansion strategy among all Chinese brands. In H1 2026, Dreame ranked among the top three in robot vacuum market share in Germany, France, and South Korea. Its revenue per square meter at German retailer MediaMarkt now exceeds that of iRobot.

### Ecovacs: Struggling Through Transition

Ecovacs experienced a difficult transition period in 2025-2026. Once the undisputed market leader, Ecovacs bet on the roller-based live-water mopping technology for its flagship Deebot X8 Pro Omni. While the OZMO ROLLER system demonstrated genuine mopping superiority, this differentiation failed to translate into market share growth.

**Why:** The X8's starting price of ¥5,999 positioned it above the mainstream flagship price band, limiting volume. Meanwhile, Ecovacs' product refresh cadence in the mid-range segment lagged behind Roborock and Dreame.

**Positive signal:** Ecovacs' commercial cleaning robot business is showing early promise. The DEEBOT Pro K series achieved approximately 40% year-over-year growth in deployments across shopping malls and office buildings, becoming a potential second revenue curve.

### Narwal: The Niche Defender

Narwal built its brand on the first-mover advantage of "self-washing mop" technology. In 2026, it maintained an approximately 8% market share by refusing to participate in the suction power war or aggressive price competition. Instead, Narwal focuses on mopping experience and base station design refinement.

The Narwal J6, released in April 2026, shrank the base station by 30% while preserving self-cleaning and water-fill/drain functionality. This design received positive feedback from households with limited floor space.

### Xiaomi Mi Ecosystem: The Price Disruptor

The 2026 Mi Robot Vacuum (¥1,999-¥2,499) continued Xiaomi's signature high-value pricing strategy. With LDS navigation, 5,000Pa suction, and rotating mop pads, combined with native integration into Xiaomi's smart home ecosystem, it became the most competitive offering in the ¥2,000 price band.

According to supply chain sources, Mi Robot Vacuum shipments in H1 2026 grew over 35% year-over-year — one of the fastest-growing SKUs in the industry. However, Xiaomi's brand premiumization and high-end market penetration remain limited.

## Technology Trends: From Specs Arms Race to AI Competition

### Mopping Technology: Plateau Reached

In H1 2026, differentiation in mopping technology narrowed significantly. Dreame's robotic arm, Ecovacs' roller-based system, and Roborock's vibrating mop — while representing three distinct approaches — achieved closely comparable real-world cleaning results.

For consumers, any robot vacuum above ¥2,500 can handle over 95% of daily mopping needs. The marginal experience improvement from investing in higher-tier mopping has become minimal.

### AI Navigation: The Next Frontier

If 2024-2025 was about "how clean does it get," H1 2026 shifted the focus to "how intelligently does it clean."

**AI obstacle avoidance:** From structured light to 3D ToF to pure vision solutions, all top brands now achieve reliable obstacle avoidance — no wall collisions, no cable tangling, no pet waste smearing. The next competitive dimension: Can the robot adapt cleaning strategy based on floor material? Can it identify different stain types and select the appropriate cleaning mode?

**AI voice interaction:** DreameGPT and Ecovacs' YIKO-GPT represent two approaches. DreameGPT is built on a general LLM architecture — essentially a "ChatGPT for robot vacuums" — while YIKO-GPT emphasizes deep integration with hardware control. Both point toward the same direction: natural-language dialogue is replacing fixed voice commands as the standard interaction method.

**Smart home ecosystem integration:** Xiaomi's Mi brand holds a natural advantage here — the Mi Ecosystem's 600 million connected devices provide rich trigger-and-action scenarios. "Auto-clean when the last person leaves home" and "start vacuuming when the air purifier turns on" are increasingly popular use cases.

### New Form Factors: Base Station Convergence and Design Innovation

All-in-one base stations (self-cleaning + auto-emptying + water fill/drain) accounted for over 65% of shipments in H1 2026. Full automation has evolved from a premium option to a baseline expectation.

Beyond the standard circular form factor, several form-factor experiments emerged in H1 2026:

- **Dreame X50 Ultra** tested a slimmer chassis design (9.8cm height), sacrificing some water tank capacity for low-clearance navigation capability
- **Ecovacs** is testing a rectangular "mopping-first, vacuuming-second" form factor targeting large tiled floor scenarios
- **Roborock** demonstrated a "split mopping and vacuuming" concept — independent vacuum and mop modules that can operate or switch independently — though this has not entered mass production

## H2 2026 Outlook

The robot vacuum industry is expected to see the following trends in the second half of 2026:

**1. Further price compression.** Average selling prices are expected to drop below ¥2,500 by end-2026, with fully-loaded products (all-in-one base station + AI obstacle avoidance) appearing at the ¥2,000 price point.

**2. AI interaction as core brand differentiator.** All major brands will launch enhanced voice interaction systems following the DreameGPT/YIKO-GPT model. Display-free interaction with intelligent planning AI will become standard on high-end models.

**3. Overseas competition intensifies.** As tariffs and logistics costs rise, brand strategies will shift from "channel coverage" to "brand building," making localization capability the decisive competitive factor.

**4. Commercial cleaning as second curve.** Both Ecovacs and Dreame have already deployed commercial cleaning robot products. More brands are expected to follow in H2.

**5. Category expansion.** Roborock and Dreame have both been reported to be developing "window cleaning robots" and "lawn mowing robots" — attempting to horizontally replicate their base station technology and AI algorithms into adjacent verticals.
