### 1. 🏦 Unitree Confirms an Aug 19 STAR Market Listing: ¥150.80 Offer Price, Roughly ¥60.99B Implied Market Value, ¥6.099B in Proceeds — China's First Humanoid-Robot Stock Enters Its Public-Market Test

**In one sentence**: _Unitree Technology announced that its shares will begin trading on the Shanghai Stock Exchange's STAR Market on Aug 19, 2026. The offering price is ¥150.80 per share, implying a market value of roughly ¥60.99 billion; 40.4464 million shares will be issued, equal to 10% of post-IPO share capital, with total proceeds expected at about ¥6.099 billion._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: Unitree's listing is about more than the 'first stock' label. It puts a robot company's revenue, profit, R&D spending and application mix into a public-market pricing process for the first time. Its prospectus shows revenue rising from ¥159 million in 2023 to ¥1.699 billion in 2025, with 2025 net profit reaching ¥278 million; first-quarter 2026 revenue was about ¥423 million, up 68.49% year on year. The pressure point is valuation: the offering P/E is 219.23x versus an industry average of 38.56x. The next question is not simply how the stock trades on day one, but whether the company can keep balancing delivery scale, R&D intensity and real-world deployment.

**Key facts**:
- **Listing date**: Aug 19, 2026, on the Shanghai Stock Exchange STAR Market
- **Offering data**: ¥150.80 per share; roughly ¥60.99B implied market value; 40.4464M new shares, 10% of post-IPO capital
- **Use of proceeds**: about ¥6.099B gross; ¥2.022B earmarked for intelligent-robot model R&D, close to half the total
- **Operating data**: 2023-2025 revenue of ¥159M, ¥393M and ¥1.699B; 2025 net profit of ¥278M; Q1 2026 revenue about ¥423M, up 68.49%
- **Valuation pressure**: 219.23x offering P/E versus a 38.56x industry average

> 📌 **Conclusion**: Unitree moves the question from 'can robots sell?' to 'can a robot company be priced in public markets over time?' The valuation anchor is landing; the next test is delivery, profit and application mix.

### 2. 🦸 Unitree's 'Superman' Robot Appears: 2-Meter Standing Jump, 12.66m/s Top Speed — A New Performance Peak on the Eve of the IPO

**In one sentence**: _On the eve of its listing, Unitree released a 'Superman' robot video showing a 2-meter standing jump and a top speed of 12.66 m/s, or roughly 45.6 km/h — faster than Usain Bolt's average speed over a 100-meter race._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: 'Superman' is better read as a statement of technical capability than as a fully commercialized product launch. Standing jumps and high-speed running stress actuator peak power, dynamic balance, control loops and structural reliability. They move the conversation from 'can it walk?' to 'can it control extreme dynamic states?' But a peak-performance clip is still far from a mass-market product. Durability, continuous operating time, energy consumption, recovery after falls and cost will determine whether the capability can enter factories or service environments.

**Key facts**:
- **Jumping ability**: 2-meter standing jump
- **Speed**: 12.66 m/s, roughly 45.6 km/h
- **Technical meaning**: actuator power density, dynamic balance, motion control and structural strength
- **Validation boundary**: a single extreme demonstration does not replace long-duration reliability, energy and cost validation

> 📌 **Conclusion**: Extreme performance creates attention; sustained reliability creates orders. The key follow-up is whether 'Superman' capability can become repeatable, maintainable and deliverable product performance.

### 3. 🤖 World Robot Conference Runs Aug 19-23 in Beijing: 150+ New Products, with 'Can It Do Real Work?' as the Core Test

**In one sentence**: _The 2026 World Robot Conference will run in Beijing from Aug 19 to 23, with more than 300 exhibitors expected and over 150 new products making their debut. Preview coverage puts 'can it do useful work?' ahead of 'can it perform on stage?'_

> 💡 **Why it matters**: The focus of industry exhibitions is changing. Dance, combat and high-speed motion still attract attention, but orders are decided by grasping success rates, task completion time, labor-substitution cost, maintenance and scenario ROI. The conference will also connect with Beijing's robot consumption street and the E-Town Robot Consumption Festival, taking robots from industry showcase to consumer and channel validation. Product launches are only the starting line; pilots, repeat purchases and batch deployment after the show are harder evidence.

**Key facts**:
- **Dates and location**: Aug 19-23, Beijing
- **New-product scale**: 150+ debuts
- **Exhibitor scale**: more than 300 companies expected in preview materials
- **Focus areas**: industrial robotics, commercial service robots, consumer robots and embodied-AI deployment

> 📌 **Conclusion**: The most important question this year is not 'who looks most human?' but 'who can finish the task, make the economics work and deliver service?'

### 4. 🏭 China's Industrial-Robot Output Rose 28.5% in the First Seven Months: Manufacturing Becomes a New Sector Health Indicator

**In one sentence**: _China's industrial-robot output rose 28.5% year on year in the first seven months, according to national economic data, while equipment manufacturing and high-tech manufacturing continued to lead growth._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: Humanoid robots are still in the early phase between pilots and volume deployment, but industrial robots already form a more mature, measurable industrial base. Higher industrial-robot output points to continued automation demand and a larger supply-chain foundation for servos, reducers, controllers, machine vision and data collection. For humanoid robots, the speed of deployment may depend less on one launch-event specification than on whether the new bodies can reuse this proven engineering and delivery system.

**Key facts**:
- **Output growth**: industrial-robot output up 28.5% year on year in the first seven months
- **Industrial backdrop**: equipment manufacturing and high-tech manufacturing continued to lead
- **Why it matters**: manufacturing data is a steadier sector signal than concept-stock volatility

> 📌 **Conclusion**: The real base of robotics is still manufacturing automation. The next phase of humanoids depends on connecting to a supply chain and delivery system that already works.
