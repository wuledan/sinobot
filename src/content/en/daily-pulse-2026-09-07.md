## 1. 🏆 Headline | The "Super Moment" Debate: Three Arguments That Decide Who Gets Falsified First

**In one sentence**: _Chinese humanoid shipments exceeded 40,000 units in H1 2026, accounting for 97% of the global total — yet the commercialization timelines given by leading companies range from two years to ten. Surging shipments, divergent public statements and swinging valuations are appearing at the same time, and the debate over embodied AI's commercial closed loop now splits into three testable arguments._

> 💡 **Why it matters**: As the industry moves from "can it be built?" to "when will it make money?", disagreement shifts from technical roadmaps to timelines and pricing logic. The answers to these three arguments will directly set the pace of primary-market fundraising and the level of secondary-market valuations.

Unitree, which listed on the STAR Market on the opening day of the World Robot Conference, surged 629.44% on day one and then fell for consecutive sessions, closing at CNY 530.30 on Sep. 4 — a drawdown of more than 50% from its CNY 1,100 opening price, for a market cap of CNY 214.5 billion. In the same week, the primary market kept placing bets: IT Juzi data shows domestic embodied-AI funding totaled CNY 93.5 billion in H1 2026, up 5x year on year across 322 deals — a half-year total already exceeding the whole of 2025. The top 20 companies by funding took CNY 55 billion, or 59% of the total.

**Argument one: the timeline.** Unitree founder Wang Xingxing gives a range of "two to three years at the fastest, five to ten at the slowest," defining the "ChatGPT moment" with a verifiable metric: take a robot into any unfamiliar environment and it completes about 80% of tasks from voice or text commands — with the current bottleneck concentrated in the "last few centimeters" of action. Galbot founder Wang He is more specific: today's embodied foundation models sit roughly at the level of GPT-2 among digital-world LLMs, and by 2028 could reach GPT-3.5 to GPT-4 levels, putting the industry's "ChatGPT moment" around 2028, with tens of thousands of robots deployed across domains by the end of the 15th Five-Year Plan period. The shipment camp states its case in revenue terms: Zhiyuan targets 20,000–30,000 shipments and CNY 4–6 billion in revenue for 2026, crossing CNY 10 billion in 2027. The capacity camp is led by UBTECH, which targets 10,000 units of annual capacity in 2026 — but still posted a CNY 703 million attributable net loss last year and has yet to turn profitable.

**Argument two: the use case.** The factory camp holds today's most certain orders — UBTECH's full-size humanoids average around CNY 760,000 and serve automakers and smart manufacturing; Zhiyuan had cumulatively produced 15,000 robots by end-June 2026 and now offers a pay-per-service leasing model; Galbot won a CNY 236 million embodied-AI procurement project with CATL and a 1,000-unit partnership with Baida Precision. The stage camp collects visibility and cash flow — Unitree shipped more than 5,500 humanoids last year, with humanoid revenue of CNY 800–900 million surpassing quadruped revenue for the first time. The living-room camp has the highest expectations and the fewest deliveries — US-based 1X prices NEO at USD 20,000 or USD 499 per month, with more than 10,000 consumers paying deposits, but as of June 2026 NEO had not actually shipped and still required remote human assistance for complex tasks. Wang Xingxing's caution is worth recording: robot efficiency still trails humans, most factory orders sit in pilot and training phases, and framework agreements outnumber batch acceptances.

**Argument three: valuation.** Price these companies as hardware makers and Fanuc — the global leader in cumulative industrial-robot installations — trades at a price-to-sales ratio of roughly 10x at its peak. Price them as platforms and Unitree's IPO implied about 24.7x sales; based on its Sep. 27 close and last year's net profit of CNY 278 million, its static P/E is roughly 895x, versus an average of 38.56x for A-share general equipment manufacturing. Unitree's freely tradable float is only 7.44% of total share capital, amplifying first-week volatility. By August, six embodied-AI companies had valuations above CNY 20 billion, per 21st Century Business Herald. The premium on top is the "humanoid track premium" — and if shipment growth disappoints, that layer gets compressed.

> 📌 **Bottom line**: Embodied AI does not lack stages. Over the next two years, only companies that can keep selling robots and win repeat orders from the same customers deserve to talk about a closed loop. Timelines will be verified by deliveries, use cases by repurchases, and valuations by growth — arguments will not end at conferences, only in earnings reports.

## 2. 🌍 OpenAI commits to building humanoid robots itself: "We will definitely do it"

**In one sentence**: _On Sep. 2, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said in a podcast, for the first time, that the company will build humanoid robot bodies itself — "We will definitely do humanoid robots, and we will also develop robots in other form factors," citing how the physical world is adapted to human body structure, and adding that data-center special-purpose robots are being developed in parallel._

This is OpenAI's clearest step yet from "brain supplier" toward "body player." Previously the company's robotics involvement came mainly through models and investments; a commitment to build its own bodies now complicates its relationships with portfolio companies such as Figure and 1X. The hardware push is also hitting resistance: in July, Apple sued OpenAI for allegedly misappropriating trade secrets related to unannounced Apple products, and in August asked a federal judge to order OpenAI to stop using the claimed secrets. OpenAI responded that Apple's request is "based on misinformation and is entirely unnecessary."

> 📌 **Implication**: When an AI giant starts building bodies, competition shifts from a "model war" to a full-stack contest of model + hardware + data loop. For Chinese vendors, the meaning of the window is unchanged: build out scenario orders and repeat purchases before the giants' supply chains take shape.

## 3. ⚡ Honor's "Lightning": 8.83 seconds after 11 months, a phone maker's engineering-transfer case study

**In one sentence**: _Today's Securities Times front-page feature dissects Honor's robot "Lightning": 100m in 8.83s, 400m in 39.45s, 1,500m in 2:30 — all three beating human world records, achieved just 11 months after the project was launched in September 2025._

How did a company known for smartphones build a robot that breaks human track records? Three numbers are worth unpacking: **400 N·m** — the peak torque of Lightning's self-developed integrated joint module, above that of an ordinary family car (200–300 N·m); **4 liters per minute** — the flow rate of a liquid-cooling system embedded inside the motors, solving overheating and throttling at full sprint; **millisecond-level** — the decision speed of the high-dynamic motion-control system that judges center of mass and foot placement mid-flight, at an average speed above 10 m/s, where even half a millisecond of latency means a fall.

The deeper answer lies in the R&D paradigm: Honor's digital-twin simulation platform compressed solution screening from three months to three days, with performance curves for more than 1,000 materials directly migratable; knees must absorb impacts of about 10x the robot's own weight, and an early prototype broke its legs after 10 km of running — the materials team solved it by reusing the high-strength-metal impact methods developed for foldable-screen hinges. Phones and robots share the same underlying engineering in thermal management, multimodal perception and reliability. This is the core logic of consumer-electronics giants crossing into robotics. In Shenzhen, where Honor is based, robot-related companies now exceed 91,000 — the first Chinese city to break the 90,000 mark.

> 📌 **Implication**: Honor's 11 months is a case study in how "phone supply chain + simulation platform" compresses traditional robot development cycles. As more giants with capital, factories and ready-made scenarios enter, the first-mover window for pure startups is narrowing — a variable that deserves pricing attention beyond valuations.

## 4. 🇺🇸 First China-humanoid ETF lists on Nasdaq — overseas capital starts paying for components

**In one sentence**: _US ETF issuer Defiance ETFs launched the first US ETF focused on Chinese humanoid robots (ticker: CRO B) on Nasdaq, heavily weighting component leaders such as Leader Harmonious Drive Systems, Inovance Technology and Hengli Hydraulic; the news helped the A-share humanoid sector attract net inflows of CNY 970 million on Sep. 4._

The ETF's stock selection reveals where overseas money's attention has shifted: no longer just assemblers, but "picks-and-shovels" suppliers in reducers and servo systems. Institutional data shows Chinese humanoid shipments accounted for more than 97% of the global total in H1 2026, with the domestic supply chain entering mass production at the 10,000-unit level — as assembled-robot volumes ramp, components become the more certain beneficiaries. The ETF and the industry data form a loop: overseas capital allocates to the Chinese supply chain through index tools, which in turn brings incremental funds to the A-share components sector.

## 5. 🇩🇪 IFA 2026: Chinese robots fill Hall 25 — but beyond the stage, open questions remain

**In one sentence**: _IFA 2026 gave robotics an unprecedented position: the organizer called this year's IFA Next its "largest humanoid-robot showcase to date," with about 300 exhibitors — AGIBOT (Zhiyuan), Galbot, EngineAI, Unitree, Dobot and DEEP Robotics were all listed in the robotics lineup — plus a new "Robots on the Runway" segment letting humanoids take the stage._

Walk into Hall 25 and Unitree anchors one end while Zhiyuan sits diagonally across — Chinese robots dominate the hall's visual center. Unitree built a small boxing ring for kung-fu demos, Dobot brought a dinosaur robot, and mova had robots DJ-ing live. Behind the spectacle, STDaily recorded a more honest detail: a consumer-electronics distributor from Paris found the kung-fu demo entertaining, but when the conversation moved from "what can this robot do" to "what does this do for your business," he had no immediate answer — **he knew the robots were interesting, but did not yet know why he needed one**.

The Chinese robot companies came to IFA with a clear motive: with US market restrictions growing, Europe matters more as an overseas destination. But several admitted they have not fully figured out how to proceed — some want distributors familiar with hotel and restaurant scenarios, others think auto channels are worth approaching, and for companies just starting on Europe, even the final use case and target customer are still being explored. A second thread is functional-robot going-global: Roborock brought lawn-mowing and pool-cleaning robots, Ecovacs showed sweeping, window-cleaning, mowing and pool categories, and Dreame presented the ECHO S1 single-arm wheeled service robot — cleaning robots are extending from indoor floors to yards and pools, entering a multi-device "same household" product system.

> 📌 **Observation**: Humanoids drew the crowds at IFA; cleaning and yard robots carried the order logic. The split mirrors the domestic market exactly: performance solves "being seen," function solves "being needed."

## 6. ⚠️ ASD posts two limit-ups, then admits the truth: embodied-AI revenue of just CNY 137,200

**In one sentence**: _After two consecutive limit-up days triggered by disclosure of its cooperation with Zhiyuan Innovation, ASD (Aishida) rushed out a risk warning on Sunday: its embodied-AI robot cooperation project is still in early stages, having generated only CNY 137,200 in recognized revenue, while its H1 net loss deepened significantly._

The announcement is a perfect footnote to the A-share embodied-AI theme rally: on one side, two limit-ups and inflows of sector capital; on the other, a listed company's disclosure of under CNY 140,000 in real revenue. The announcement offers the most direct comparison for judging whether the theme rally is froth. For investors, at a time when embodied-AI valuations are broadly rising, wording like "revenue already recognized" deserves closer reading than concept labels — it quantifies the gap between "telling a story" and "having revenue" in concrete numbers.
